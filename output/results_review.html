Biomarker Prediction Results

Twenty blood metals and nutritional biomarkers add nothing to the prediction of low cognitive performance beyond age, education and income. The result is precise rather than underpowered, it survives every pre-specified sensitivity analysis, and the biomarker panel is significantly worse than eight questions a clinician can ask without drawing blood.

Primary result · delta-AUC (M3 minus M0), L2-penalized logistic
−0.0019
bootstrap 95% CI [−0.0108, +0.0071]  ·  n = 1,784  ·  431 events

The interval includes zero, so H1 is not supported. The informative part is its upper bound: +0.0071 sits well below the 0.02 meaningful-difference threshold fixed in advance. The data rule out an improvement large enough to matter, which is a stronger claim than failing to reach significance, and it is only available because the threshold was pre-specified.

The sharper finding

A twenty-analyte lab panel is beaten by eight clinical questions

M0+ is the pre-specified secondary baseline: demographics plus BMI, smoking, alcohol, PHQ-9 depression, stroke, diabetes, hypertension and high cholesterol. It reaches AUC 0.8023 against 0.7884 for demographics alone, while the full biomarker model reaches 0.7865.

04

The mixture-method benchmark

The computational contribution: quantile g-computation, the method this literature relies on, reimplemented in Python with training-fold quantile cutpoints so it fits inside the CV loop without leakage, then scored on predictive rather than inferential criteria.

ModelCross-validated AUC
Demographics only (M0)0.7884
Quantile g-computation, metals mixture + demographics0.7874
M1, same metals as ordinary features + demographics0.7868

psi = −0.1162 joint log-odds per simultaneous one-quantile increase, with cadmium carrying 0.820 of the positive weight. The mixture method performs no better than demographics alone, and no better than entering the same metals as ordinary features. A technique built for inferential decomposition of a mixture confers no predictive advantage. BKMR was excluded in advance on convergence grounds for binary outcomes; WQS was deferred and now has no basis for inclusion.

The three demographics are rank-stable to the point of determinism. Every biomarker swings across a 15 to 20 rank interval between resamples. That instability is a finding in itself: single-run SHAP rankings over correlated biomarkers are not reproducible, which is a direct caution for a literature that reports them without resampling.

06

Robustness

  • The imputation analysis forecloses the obvious objection. Option B, the full labelled sample of 3,124 with multiple imputation fitted inside each fold, gives delta-AUC +0.0015 with CI [−0.0030, +0.0063]. That is the tightest interval of any analysis and still null, so the complete-case sampling decision did not create the null.
  • Decision-curve analysis agrees. M3 exceeds M0 at 21 of 56 thresholds, indistinguishable from chance, with a maximum net-benefit gain of +0.0059. There is no threshold at which the panel would change a decision.
  • Removing the metals block slightly improves discrimination (−0.0028, CI [−0.0051, −0.0004]). The metals contribute noise, not signal.
  • The U-shape rationale fails on its own terms. Partial dependence for selenium and manganese shows no U, with extrema near the ends of the range and a total swing of about 0.05 in predicted probability.
  • One result points marginally positive, reported for completeness. On the continuous composite, cross-validated R-squared rises from 0.3825 to 0.3908, a gain of +0.0083, about 0.8% of additional variance. It does not translate into discrimination for the binary outcome and is far too small to change the conclusion.
07

What this means, and the venue question

Against the brief's own decision framework this is Scenario C on the primary axis combined with Scenario E on the secondary. Both were pre-written as legitimate publishable outcomes, and Scenario C is described there as the cautionary result and arguably the most interesting one.

The claim

Seven published studies report significant associations between these biomarkers and cognition in this exact population and cycle range. Translating those associations into individual-level prediction adds essentially nothing beyond age, education and income. Three findings carry it past a bare null: the null is precise rather than underpowered, the panel is significantly worse than routine clinical covariates, and the mixture method the literature depends on confers no predictive advantage either.

A decision for both authors

The brief routes Scenario C to Diagnostic and Prognostic Research, Journal of Clinical Epidemiology or PLOS ONE, and warns that Scenario E weakens an IEEE framing. That tension is now real.

The argument for keeping IEEE: the contribution was reframed as a reusable evaluation protocol for testing whether reported epidemiological associations confer predictive utility. A precise null arguably demonstrates that protocol better than a positive result would, and sections 04 and 05 above are genuine computational content rather than a standard classifier comparison.

No causal language applies. These data are cross-sectional. Nothing here says any biomarker does or does not affect cognition; the finding is strictly about predictive utility and the gap between association and prediction. The outcome throughout is low cognitive performance, never cognitive impairment.
