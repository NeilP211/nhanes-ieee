02

Three decisions

These are yours and your co-author's to make, not mine, because each trades sample size against construct coverage. My recommendation is on each, with the reasoning that would change it.

Decision 1

Keep or drop CUSEZN, the serum trace minerals

  • The problem. Serum copper, selenium and zinc were measured in a one-third subsample of ages 6+, in both cycles, requiring the WTSA2YR weight. The brief lists it as an ordinary Block 3 file. Requiring it would cut the analytic n to roughly a third.
  • What dropping it actually costs. Only copper and zinc. Selenium is already in Block 2 as whole-blood LBXBSE, on the full sample. So the trace-mineral construct is not lost, it is thinned.
  • What keeping it costs. An analytic n near 700 instead of 2,100. At that size the brief's own guidance says drop MLP and KNN, and the delta-AUC confidence intervals widen enough that a modest true effect becomes undetectable.
Recommend: drop from the main analysis, keep as a supplement. Run M0 to M3 on the full sample without copper and zinc, then repeat on the CUSEZN subsample as a secondary analysis. You get the headline n and the trace-mineral question, reported honestly as two different samples. Reverse this only if copper and zinc are central to your co-author's literature framing.
Decision 2

Keep or drop TRIGLY, the triglycerides and LDL file

  • The problem. Measured in a fasting subsample of ages 12+, weight WTSAF2YR. This is exactly the objection the brief uses to exclude the serum fatty acids file, so including TRIGLY is internally inconsistent.
  • There is a clean workaround. BIOPRO carries triglycerides as LBXSTR on the full sample. Total cholesterol is in TCHOL and HDL in HDL, both full sample. So dropping TRIGLY costs only LDL.
  • And LDL is derived anyway. NHANES computes it by Friedewald from total cholesterol, HDL and triglycerides, which is only valid fasting. A non-fasting LDL would not be trustworthy even if we could construct it.
Recommend: drop TRIGLY, take triglycerides from BIOPRO. Net loss is LDL alone, and the lipid block stays on the full sample. This one I would take unless your co-author specifically needs LDL in the discussion.
Decision 3

Add DIQ for diabetes, or drop diabetes as a covariate

  • The problem. The brief lists MCQ as covering "stroke, diabetes". Stroke is there, as MCQ160F. Diabetes is not in MCQ at all, it is DIQ010 in DIQ_G and DIQ_H, a 23rd file the brief never names.
  • The file is cheap. Full sample, both cycles present, 57 and 58 variables, and we only need one or two of them.
  • Partial redundancy. We already have HbA1c as LBXGH, which is objective glycemia. DIQ010 adds self-reported diagnosis, a genuinely different construct: it captures treatment and awareness, not just glucose control.
Recommend: add DIQ. The cost is one more merge, and diabetes is too well established a correlate of cognitive performance to leave out on a technicality. I have not downloaded it yet, so its variable names are codebook-verified only.

04

Running the phases consecutively

You asked whether the phases can run without stops. Mostly yes. Two gates are load-bearing and cannot be automated away, but the other five can be collapsed. The honest structure is two stops instead of seven, achieved by front-loading the decisions rather than removing them.

Gate 1 · you are here

Lock the variable list and answer the three decisions

Genuinely blocking. The brief calls re-cleaning after a variable change the most expensive avoidable event in the project, and it is right: a changed variable list invalidates the merge, the outcome, and every model downstream. This is also the joint written sign-off the brief requires. Cost of getting it wrong: about a week.

then runs unattended

Phase 1
Cohort
src/01_cohort.py
Download the remaining files, merge on SEQN, apply per-variable reserved codes, handle below-LOD flags, build the attrition table. Deterministic once the variable list is locked.
Phase 2
Outcome
src/02_outcome.py
Z-score each test within the pooled 60+ sample, average with the 3-of-4 rule, cut the lowest quartile. Emits distributions and an internal consistency check. Safe to chain onto Phase 1: it depends only on the cleaned file, and its outputs are diagnostics you review at the next gate rather than decisions you make during it.
Gate 2 · the one that cannot move

Review the diagnostics pack, then date and sign the pre-registration

This gate is scientific, not procedural. The pre-registration must be dated before anyone sees an outcome-predictor relationship, or the pre-specification claim is void. That claim is load-bearing: it is what TRIPOD+AI reporting rests on, and it is the entire defence in section 6 of the brief against searching for a specification that produces a positive result. Chain through this gate and the paper's central methodological argument goes with it.

It is also the last cheap moment to catch a cleaning error, before it is baked into every model.

then runs unattended to completion

Phase 3
Model
src/03_model.py
M0 to M3 across penalized logistic, random forest and XGBoost. Stratified 10-fold, repeated 5x, every transform fitted inside the fold.
Phase 4
Evaluate
src/04_evaluate.py
AUC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration, decision curves, and the headline bootstrap delta-AUCs against M0.

Bootstrapped SHAP rankings, partial dependence for selenium and manganese, plus the quantile g-computation benchmark.
Phase 6
Sensitivity
src/06_sensitivity.py
All five pre-specified sensitivity analyses. Pure reruns of a locked pipeline, which is why this is the most chainable stretch in the project.

Why phases 3 to 6 chain safely

Once the dataset is locked and the analysis is pre-registered, these four phases contain no decisions, only computation. Every choice they encode was made at Gate 2. The brief budgets the entire modeling and sensitivity workload at under two hours of compute, so this is one unattended run, not a campaign.

What chaining requires in exchange

Removing review stops means errors travel further before anyone sees them. That is a real cost and it is paid up front, in guardrails:

  • Assertions that abort the chain. Row counts after every merge, SEQN uniqueness, no reserved code surviving into a numeric column, no column silently all-missing, weights summing to a plausible population total. A chained pipeline without these is just an unsupervised one.
  • The leakage check becomes a test, not a review step. The brief calls leakage the failure mode that silently ruins the project. If nobody is reviewing between phases, that check has to fail the build automatically rather than wait for a human to look.
  • Every phase still writes to data/interim/. So a failure at Phase 5 resumes from Phase 4 instead of restarting the merge.
  • One diagnostics pack per gate. The point of collapsing stops is fewer, better reviews, not less review. Each gate gets a single document, the way Phase 0 produced one audit.
  • One thing to change on your side. CLAUDE.md currently says to stop at the end of every phase and not chain them. If you want this structure, that instruction needs rewriting, otherwise I will keep stopping. Your call, your file.

Net effect on the timeline: the brief budgets 7 to 9 weeks. Consolidating the gates does not speed up the compute, which was never the bottleneck. It removes roughly a week of handoff latency, and it concentrates your review attention into two substantial passes rather than seven shallow ones.
